# Capstone project brief

A group project that closes the course: each group picks one of the five case studies in [`../cases/`](../cases/),
analyses it and presents a recommendation.

## Why a capstone

- **Apply what was taught:** the case brings the course topics (ERP, WMS, TMS, 3PL/4PL, demand planning) together in
  one decision.
- **Work like in a company:** groups are assigned, not chosen, so students practise working with colleagues they did
  not pick.
- **Something to show:** the presentation is a piece for the students' portfolio.

## Process (about twelve teaching weeks)

| Week | Step |
|---|---|
| 1 | Groups are formed |
| 2–4 | Each group chooses one of the five cases and names a group lead |
| 5–8 | Project development: requirements, options, analysis, recommendation |
| 9–11 | Slides finished and rehearsed |
| last week | 20-minute presentation per group, then questions; slides are handed in the same day |

## Assessment criteria

- **Depth of knowledge:** how well did the group explore its topic?
- **Creativity and originality:** is the solution innovative?
- **Quality of execution:** how well did the group develop and present the project?
- **Relevance and impact:** can fellow students understand the work?

Weighting used in the course: 30 % for the submitted slides, 40 % for the presentation in class, 30 % for in-class
participation.

## Optional: combine with the practice data

The practice data in [`../data/`](../data/README.md) were made for a separate group exercise on big data and
data-driven decisions. They can be combined with any case, for example to back a recommendation with a demand
forecast, an inventory check against reorder point and safety stock, or a supplier comparison by lead time.
